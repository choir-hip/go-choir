# Paper chunk drain: cause, rate, options (read-only, 2026-11-12 ~20:25Z)

Tags: [O] observed (source), [I] inferred, [?] open. Nothing was changed on any host or in the repo. No file was written on Node B.

## 1. Cause (confidence: high on the mechanism, medium on why the attempts keep failing)

The snapshot producer re-runs a full-store snapshot attempt about every 30 minutes because no attempt ever completes into a recorded snapshot, so its "tail" trigger (540,000 events) stays due. Every attempt re-uploads nearly every chunk of the state file, because a fixed 4 MiB page-range cut shares almost nothing across 30 minutes of writes to a 21 GiB btree store. Failed attempts leave their chunks in no root, so the collector holds them 24 h.

### Chain of evidence

1. Chunking is fixed-size and deterministic. [O] internal/snapshot/snapshot.go:20-30, 61-62: the cut is `ChunkSize = 4 << 20` (1024 pages of 4 KiB), and `filecas.SealChunk` is deterministic (internal/filecas/filecas.go:200-215, a synthetic nonce over the plaintext, zstd, so avg sealed chunk is about 0.6 MiB). There is no per-snapshot nonce or key, so identical pages give an identical digest. Proof on the host [O]: the two snapshot roots the platform holds for the paper (control DB, computer_snapshot_roots; created 18:55:55Z and 19:18:21Z, both head_sequence 10914341) share 10510 of 10510 chunks. Dedup works when the store is frozen. The wedge froze it.
2. A snapshot root has 10,510 chunks: about 5,337 for the state file (22,382,874,624 B / 4 MiB, from the store dump) plus about 5,170 of telemetry partitions (closed partitions are stable bytes). [I split, O total]
3. When the store is live, about 88 percent of the state-file chunks are new each attempt. [O] births (stat birth time, not mtime) of the paper's chunk files: 08Z 5.5k, then 8.9k to 9.5k per hour every hour to 14Z, 8.9k at 15Z. That is about 4.7k new files per 30-minute attempt, against about 5.3k state chunks. [O] the guest WAL pinned by the stream grew to 1.99 GB at 15:12Z (console log) and 3.67 GB at 20:21Z. Hundreds of thousands of page writes land across 5.3k chunks, so almost every chunk is dirty. A fixed cut cannot dedup that. The code comments already say so (snapshot_producer.go:60-64, snapshot_roots.go:30-35: "changes most of its page ranges between snapshots").
4. Why attempts repeat every 30 minutes. [O] bursts of 4.5k-6k chunk files appear at 08:39, 09:39, 10:09, 10:39 and so on (per-minute mtime and birth histograms). The upload of an attempt takes 3 to 4 minutes at the start. The witness then runs inside the same read transaction for 20 minutes or more (goroutine dump 19:14Z: the capture goroutine is in `LiveStoreWitnessTx` -> `queryDigest`, and the four `chunkUploader` workers have been in `chan receive` for 18 minutes). `snapshotProducer.run` (internal/agentcore/snapshot_producer.go:173-250) sets `lastSeq` and `lastAt` only on success (lines 244-247). Any error leaves the tail due, and the next 10-minute tick (`snapshotTickEvery`, line 79) starts a new full attempt. There is no backoff and no memory of the failed attempt.
5. What the failures are. [O] guest console logs on the host (/var/lib/go-choir/vm-state/vm-1669020a.../console.log*, covering 14:53Z to now; older logs are rotated away):
   - 16:11:59Z `runtime: snapshot failed (retry in 10m0s): snapshot: append snapshot_recorded: computer event projection repair required`. This is the appender wedge: the capture finished and the root was registered (the platform shows roots at 18:55 and 19:18 at the frozen head), but the event could not be appended. Cycle about 22-23 min.
   - 20:21:46Z (after the 19:18Z refresh and the 19:45Z host restart) `runtime: snapshot failed (retry in 10m0s): aborted at the 30m0s time budget (CHOIR_SNAPSHOT_MAX_DURATION) with nothing recorded: snapshot: witness of the snapshot: snapshot: exceeded the time budget`, WAL 3,665,889,512 bytes, `wal_checkpoint` took 1m21s. No root is registered on a budget abort (TakeSnapshot registers after capture), but the chunks are already on the host.
   - No "snapshot taken" line exists anywhere in 14:53Z to 20:22Z.
   - [I] The 08Z to 15Z attempts were budget aborts too: the capture ends at 15:12:21 and 15:42:34 (30:13 apart, WAL 2.0 GB then 0.74 GB) and the bursts are exactly 30 minutes apart at 09:39, 10:09 and 10:39. Exactly 30 minutes is the budget plus the buffered tick. The 08Z to 14:53Z console logs are gone, so this part is inference. The separate read-only check that would settle it is the guest log from 08:30Z to 14:50Z (the observability recent_log ring does not reach back that far; the console.log.5 and older files were rotated away), or the `snapshot_recorded` events on the paper's tape between 08Z and 15Z (none expected).
6. Nothing in the snapshot, filecas, store WAL or witness code changed since 2026-11-07 (`git log --since=2026-11-04` on internal/snapshot, internal/filecas, internal/computerversion, snapshot_producer.go, restore_snapshot.go, store/wal_bound.go and telemetry_partition.go shows only c0e2e440e, a desk-name refactor). The deploy of 26f9d66f3 (2026-11-11T21:35Z) shows no snapshot-related change. So this is not a regression. [I] The first attempt at 08:39Z was the first time the 540k-event tail came due since the last successful snapshot (the 11-11 18.7 GB burst), and it has not completed since. The trigger for the failure is that the witness pass plus the 3.5-minute stream no longer fits in 30 minutes on a 3.4M-row og_objects store under load. In the quiet wedge it took about 22 minutes.

### The measurement artifact in the problem record

HEAD on a chunk is a claim: `ClaimFileChunk` does `os.Chtimes(now)` on every held chunk (internal/platform/file_cas_blobs.go:91-92; file_cas_http.go:36-39, 54-70). So every attempt, new or not, touches the mtime of up to 10.5k held chunks. Therefore:
- 16Z and 17Z: no attempts ran (no chunk activity at all), consistent with the wedge.
- 18Z mtime 3.5 GiB and 19Z mtime 6.9 GiB are mostly touches: birth times show 4 new files in the 18Z hour (the 18:56-18:58 burst of 5,722 files / 3.55 GiB is 100 percent claims of already-held chunks; both retained roots share all 10,510 chunks). So the wedge did not drain the disk. The real births resumed at 19:49Z after the restart (7,288 new files, 4.23 GiB, born after 19:40Z).
- The 49 GiB free-space drop from 08Z to 16Z is real: births 08Z to 15Z total about 41 GiB of new chunk files (sum of the hourly births 2.9 + 6.0 + 6.1 + 6.2 + 6.1 + 6.1 + 6.2 + about 2 GiB).

- Rate while the loop runs against a loaded store: 6.1 GiB/h of births (08Z to 15Z: 6.0 to 6.2 GiB every hour), 5.9 GiB/h after the 19:45Z restart (4.23 GiB in 43 minutes). Nothing from the loop is collectable before about 08:40Z tomorrow, so the net drain is about 6.2 GiB/h including the steady 0.3 GiB/h of tape, images and capture.
- Time to the 60 GiB floor if the loop continues: 26 / 6.2 = about 4.2 hours (range 3.5 to 5 hours, so around 00:30Z to 01:30Z). At the 12-hour average of 4.4 GiB/h the figure is 5.9 hours. Time to empty disk: another 10 to 14 hours. Peak steady state of the loop would be 24 h x 6 GiB = about 145 GiB of unrooted chunks, which does not fit.
- The loop ends by itself only if one attempt completes in 30 minutes (then `lastSeq` advances and the tail is not due again for about 540k events, roughly a day at the paper's 6 events/s average). The post-restart load (WAL 3.7 GB in 30 minutes) is temporary catch-up work, so this can happen, but attempts of the last 4 hours show 0 of about 10 completing as a recorded snapshot.

## 3. Options, ranked

### A. Durable fix (guest, internal/agentcore/snapshot_producer.go). Mutation class: red (snapshot/restore, silent-failure risk). Rollback: revert. Expedite: internal/agentcore is on the deploy expedite list.
1. A failed attempt must not make the next tick re-upload the store. Record the head sequence and the failure at `run` (the err path at lines 232-243) and make the due test (lines 213-224) require the tail to grow by a further fraction since the failed attempt, or back off exponentially from the 10-minute tick to hours, and say it in the log. This is a backoff on our own store work, not a limit on model calls. It turns 6 GiB/h into one attempt per backoff step.
2. Do not upload before the attempt can finish. Run the witness (or measure the page scan) first, then stream and upload, or compute the witness during the page scan in one pass. This turns a budget abort into a zero-byte abort (internal/snapshot/snapshot.go `capture`, lines 436-507: the stream at 457-462, then the witness at 465-472).
3. The deeper issue is that a fixed 4 MiB cut cannot dedup a 21 GiB btree store written at this rate. Dedup is only real while the store is frozen. A content-defined or page-diff snapshot (or an incremental page log since the last snapshot) is the structural change. That is a design item, not a hotfix.
4. The witness cost (20 to 30 minutes over 3.4M og_objects rows in one read transaction, pinning the WAL to 3.7 GB) is its own problem. Do not raise the budget to compensate. A longer pin grows the WAL on the guest data disk (3.7 GB per 30 minutes now).

Also record the problem (CLAUDE.md "Problems come first"): the 11-12 problem record's belief section should change from "chunking defeats dedup, cause unknown" to the chain above, and the "wedge drained the disk" statement should be corrected (touches, not births).

### B. Immediate relief, owner-only. I did not perform any of these.
1. Let an attempt complete. Mechanism: `CHOIR_SNAPSHOT_MAX_DURATION` is a deadline already in the code (snapshot_producer.go:55, default 30m). Raising it to about 60 minutes lets the 22-to-35-minute witness finish and records a snapshot, which stops the loop for about a day. Effect: roughly 6 GiB/h stops. Risk: needs the guest env and a planned restart (resume-once marker), the read transaction pins the WAL for up to 60 minutes (the WAL was 3.67 GB after 30 minutes under today's load, so up to about 7 GB), and the producer will then spend 3.5 GiB of chunk writes for the one good snapshot. The attempt that completes can also hit the append wedge again if it recurs. Evidence the knob is needed: the 20:21:46Z log line above.
2. Reclaim unrooted chunks now. The 24-hour window is a constant (artifact_gc.go:173) and the on-demand endpoint clamps the grace to at least the configured policy (`clampArtifactGCRequest`, lines 364-395). A request cannot go below 24 hours. Options: (a) deploy a change to the window (not possible while the floor is the deploy gate and the loop runs; 26 GiB of headroom is enough for one deploy now, but the deploy must not wait); (b) a hand deletion of the paper's chunk files that are not named by the two snapshot roots in computer_snapshot_roots, not named by the latest file root, and whose mtime is older than, say, 2 hours (so no in-flight upload or claim is touched). This frees up to about 45 GiB immediately (80k files). Mutation class black (irreversible, destructive on production). Risk: a chunk that an in-flight attempt claimed with HEAD and that is deleted before the attempt registers its root makes the root refused or the snapshot fail loudly (file_cas_http.go and the artifact_gc comment on line 167-169: "the host refuses a root that names a chunk it does not hold"), then the next attempt re-uploads. Do not use `PLATFORM_ARTIFACT_GC_GRACE`: it also shortens the file-root retirement window (the owner's file history, `retireFileCASRoots`).
3. Pause the producer with `CHOIR_SNAPSHOT_DISABLE=1` (the code labels it the emergency override). It stops the loop immediately but leaves the paper with no snapshot newer than 11-11 and a long replay tail (the recovery bound is 720k events). Not recommended while the tape tail is already past 540k events.

Recommended order: the owner decides B1 (the single action that stops the drain), while A1 and A2 land as the durable fix. Do B2 only if the floor is within about 2 hours and B1 has not stopped the births.
