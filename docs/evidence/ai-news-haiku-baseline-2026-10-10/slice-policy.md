## Items inside the window (most important first)

### 1. Trump names DNI Jay Clayton to lead a new "Super Intelligence Force" AI task force
- **Date:** 2026-10-04 (Trump's social-media announcement on Sunday; ABC article stamped Oct 4, 11:53 AM)
- **Substance:** The White House task force will coordinate federal engagement with consumers, public-interest and religious groups, critical-infrastructure providers and "Super Intelligence Companies." Trump named Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton to lead it while he stays DNI, alongside FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon R&E Under Secretary Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor. Clayton is also reported as the White House AI czar, and he told WSJ the group would examine current law and discuss possible actions for Congress. Reporting says the force has no statutory authority and no dedicated budget.
- **Sources:** https://abcnews.com/Politics/president-donald-trump-announces-creation-super-intelligence-force/story?id=136986122 ; https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/10/04/trump-names-national-intelligence-director-jay-clayton-lead-new-federal-ai-task-force/ ; https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/20779/trump-jay-clayton-super-intelligence-force-ai-czar
- **Confidence:** High that the announcement happened. Medium on membership and powers: sources disagree on whether Ferguson is a member and on whether the AI czar post is new or the one David Sacks held.

### 2. Sen. Maria Cantwell releases a six-point frontier AI safety framework (no bill text)
- **Date:** 2026-10-07
- **Substance:** Cantwell, the top Democrat on Senate Commerce, released six principles: enforceable federal frontier safety standards; continuous testing and audit; transparency and incident reporting; public-private partnerships on defensive AI; protections for people (children, workers, human oversight of consequential decisions); and international cooperation, including a rapid communication channel with China for dangerous control failures. The framework gives NIST a central role and calls for independent audits before covered models are released, plus tester access to models and training data. A committee aide says it has no legislative text yet.
- **Sources:** https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/10/07/top-senate-commerce-democrat-releases-six-point-ai-framework/ ; https://rollcall.com/2026/10/07/sen-maria-cantwell-looks-to-federal-testing-role-in-ai-safety-framework/ ; https://political.org/2026/10/07/sen-maria-cantwell-unveils-six-point-framework-for-federal-ai-regulation/
- **Confidence:** High.

### 3. Congress leaves for the midterms with AI bills unpassed; Cruz blocks unanimous-consent requests
- **Date:** Reported in weekly roundups dated Oct 4 to 5. The underlying blocked requests were Sept 29 to 30.
- **Substance:** Sen. Ted Cruz blocked unanimous consent for the AI Risk Management and Security Act (Sens. Warner, Schatz, Kim; 45-day pre-release model access and an AI Safety Board in Commerce) and objected to Sen. Blackburn's NO FAKES Act. The Ratepayer Protection Act (House passed 417-3 on Sept 16) failed a Senate cloture vote 57-43 on Sept 30, short of 60. Newly introduced or announced bills include the Protecting Kids from Human-Like Chatbots Act (Reps. Miller-Meeks, Whitesides, Kennedy, Matsui), the Data Center Transparency Act (Sens. Curtis and Blunt Rochester; federal reporting for sites of 50 MW or more) and a Banks-Gillibrand bill (S. 5601) on DoD reporting for large AI contractors. The House is not expected back until after the Nov 3 midterms, and the roundup judges AI legislation unlikely to pass this year.
- **Sources:** https://www.dcaidecoded.com/p/october-5-this-week-in-ai-federal ; https://rollcall.com/2026/09/30/voluntary-ratepayer-protection-bill-blocked-in-senate-again/ ; https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/us-senate-rejects-bill-targeting-ai-data-centre-electricity-costs
- **Confidence:** Medium. Mostly from aggregator roundups, and most new bills have no bill numbers.

### 4. Sen. Patty Murray convenes a Washington state AI roundtable and rejects self-regulation
- **Date:** 2026-10-08 (Spokesman-Review, updated Oct 8). The photo caption says the event was Thursday Oct 9, which conflicts with the dateline.
- **Substance:** In Olympia, Murray convened state policymakers (staff from Gov. Bob Ferguson's and AG Nick Brown's offices) and industry experts. She said "Right now, there is virtually no regulation for AI at the federal level" and that the country "can't afford to regulate AI with nothing but a pinky promise," a reference to the Sept 29 voluntary industry accord. She said some principles in Cantwell's framework, released the day before, were "extremely important," but proposed no bill or funding figures. Washington has enacted three AI-related laws this year, including provenance data for AI-created recordings.
- **Sources:** https://spokesman.com/stories/2026/oct/08/sen-patty-murray-meets-with-state-policymakers-to-
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 5. Executive order renames "artificial intelligence" to "super intelligence" in federal usage; CAISI becomes CAISSI
- **Date:** Reported Oct 4 to 5. The order is dated Sept 29 in the roundups.
- **Substance:** Directs agencies to use "super intelligence" and "SI" in place of "AI" and "artificial intelligence," and gives the science adviser 60 days to propose a legal definition. NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) is renamed the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI). One aggregator gives the order number as EO 14434.
- **Sources:** https://www.originbrief.app/en/reports/ai-regulation-policy/2026-10-05/weekly ; https://www.dcaidecoded.com/p/october-5-this-week-in-ai-federal
- **Confidence:** Low-medium. The order number and text were not checked against the Federal Register.

### 6. Hawley-Murphy AI Agent Accountability Act expected; strict-liability proposals after an agent-security hearing
- **Date:** Introduction expected per the Oct 4 to 5 roundups. The hearing was Sept 30.
- **Substance:** Sens. Josh Hawley and Chris Murphy are expected to introduce the AI Agent Accountability Act, which would impose liability on AI developers and operators for reckless agent harm. At the Sept 30 Senate Homeland Security subcommittee hearing "Rogue AI: Securing the Homeland Against AI Agent Attacks," Hawley proposed strict product liability, and Georgetown's Paul Ohm urged strict liability for physical injury, death or critical-infrastructure damage.
- **Sources:** https://www.originbrief.app/en/reports/ai-regulation-policy/2026-10-05/weekly ; https://www.dcaidecoded.com/p/october-5-this-week-in-ai-federal
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. The bill has not been confirmed as introduced.
