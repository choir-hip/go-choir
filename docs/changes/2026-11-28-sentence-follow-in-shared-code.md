What: in two commits. First (S0), `RadioInfo` stops carrying `progress`, `sentenceIndex` and `sentences` across the boundary (gone from `interface/bar.json`, `exact.json` and the Swift struct's encoding; `radio-info-fields.test.ts` holds the shape to the five fields the contracts read). Second (S2+S3), the sentence the radio is saying is made in shared code, on both phones. Native reports one value in the deck's state, `place`: the entry id it was given, a digest of the text shared code handed it (length and FNV-1a over UTF-16 code units), the unit being spoken as a range into that text, and the media seconds in the unit and its length. iPhone: `ProgrammePlayer.spokenPlace` reads the segment, the block and a document generation under one lock, then the audio's position, and drops the place (after two retries) when the playhead was scheduled under another generation; ranges are found once when the segment is prepared (`DeckDigest.ranges`). Android: `DeckMediaEngine.place()` over `SpeechRanges` and `placeOf` (a negative Media3 duration is 0). Shared: `AutoradioLab/radio-follow.ts` (pure; every value read is an argument) makes the reader's follow (`rfollow`, from `readerFollowShared`) and the Autoradio page's sentence and progress (`radioPageFacts` with `ds` and the new `rair`). Lit only when the entry id, the deck's item, the sheet's on-air entry and the handed id agree and the digest matches; else nothing. Deleted: `ReaderFollow.swift` (`ReaderFollow`, `ReaderFeed`, `ReaderFollowInfo`), `BarTracker.sentences` and `sentenceIndex`, the three native-only `RadioInfo` fields, `HeldBlock.sentences` and `HeldPages.normalised`, the `sentences` of the `pageText` hand-over, the `readerFollow` op and the `sentences`, `sentenceIndex`, `progress` and `peek` of `radioFacts`. Non-voice native lines 3006 to 2894, files 32 to 31, interface names 13 to 12. Android has the follow for the first time.

Why: the follow existed only in Swift, so Android had none, and the parked attempt (docs/evidence/bar-facts-lane-parked-2026-11-27.md) showed two races when two sides own the facts. Native now says where it is, in the text shared code owns, with its own identity; shared code stamps nothing. The third and fourth slices of [the plan](../design/sentence-follow-in-shared-code-2026-11-28.md).

Proof: `radio-follow.test.ts` (F1 to F8, F10 to F16, F19, F21 to F23, written first and red until the module existed), `SentencePlaceTests` and `DeckTests` in Swift (F9, F18, F20: the same-index swap gives no place), `DeckPlaceTest` in Kotlin on the host JVM (F22; mutation of the duration guard turns it red), the whole `bun test`, the contract build, `exact test web`, `swift test` (MailSeal's K3 skipped), the gates. Not proved: any sentence timing on a device; the iPhone compile of the touched app-side Swift (AutoradioModule+Bar.swift, AutoradioModule+Reader.swift, AutoradioModule+Queue.swift, AutoradioLab/modules/apple/ModelsEntry.swift); the Android build of DeckMediaEngine.kt, DeckController.kt, DeckEngine.kt, DeckState.kt and DeckAndroid.kt (the JVM tests compile all but DeckMediaEngine.kt and DeckAndroid.kt). A device must confirm: that the sentence chosen by share of characters is the one heard; that `exo.duration` is known for the chunk on air and `currentMediaItemIndex` moves with the sound, not before; what `total 0` looks like while the first Android chunks are made; the one beat of nothing at each change of entry; and a locked or backgrounded phone.

Review round (same change): the page position is not searched for. The reported range gives the spoken sentence's offset in the entry text, and `radio-follow.ts` `locateAt` applies one rule: let K be the normalised entry sentence holding the offset, and k its number among the entry's sentences equal to K (1-based, in order); light the k-th sentence equal to K on the page if and only if the page has exactly as many sentences equal to K as the entry has, else nothing. It cannot light another copy (same count, same ordinal), costs one pass over each text, has no table and no cap. What it gives up: a sentence the page shows more or fewer times than the entry has (a page-only duplicate, a caption that repeats a sentence, a sentence cut differently) is never lit, and nor is one under 6 characters. The page's blocks and the entry's paragraphs are cut by one function (`piecesOf`; a text over the reader's size is one sentence on both sides). Measured on a 3,000-sentence document (132 KB, 30 blocks), `followOf` takes 5.3 ms warm (8 ms the first call) on this laptop and 0.33 ms for 100 sentences; no precomputed-list resource was added. In the first review round this was a longest-common-run alignment; it could light an earlier copy and was replaced. `rair` is asked again when `ds.item` changes and is refreshed with `rpfacts` by the page's gestures; the ids are checked on every ask, so a skip that reached only one of the sheet and the deck state lights nothing (tests: "skip, then the follow before the next pulse"). `DeckDigest.ranges` now matches a blank run only to a blank run. Known costs: `rair` carries the on-air entry's whole text over the bridge on each ask (the pulse and the beat, about once a second while it plays; an entry's text is bounded by the sheet's own entry bounds, radio-runtime.ts:488 "up to 8 KB"), and is not sent only on change. The page's sentence shows only for entries the radio handed to the deck as text (radio-runtime.ts:339 and :356, which includes opening, transition, answer and result entries on the sheet); a voice-turn answer spoken live by the voice engine (TurnEngine.swift:737, `sp.run`) is not a deck item, so the page shows no sentence for it, as the bar does not either.

Replaced: three fields on the wire; the Swift text match of the spoken sentence against the held page (`ReaderFollow.locate`) and the Swift sentence estimate (`BarTracker.sentences`).

Undo: revert the commit.
