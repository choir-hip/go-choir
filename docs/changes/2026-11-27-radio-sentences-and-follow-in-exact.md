What: native `radioFacts` hands over facts only, in one answer from one moment, read once a pulse by the radio (no second call): the document being spoken (native's own id), the block's number and count, its spoken words (sent whole every pulse, no cache or key), and the audio's place in it. The document, block index and words come from one read of the player (`ProgrammePlayer.place`) and the place from the position source under the same lock, and the place is 0 unless the position is for the block whose words are returned. The sentence split, the sentence the place falls in, the programme progress and the block and sentence of the page in front that the radio is saying are made in `mobile/AutoradioLab/radio-follow.ts` (cases in `radio-follow.test.ts`), on the reader's one splitter. `readerFollow` is a shared source asked again on the pulse, the page shown, the document's state and version and the account state, and a document action refreshes it. It lights only when the read is the entry on air now (the entry that was on air when it arrived), native's document is that entry's `rs:` id or the story's whole address (a revision is another address), and the page shown is the page on air. An older answer never replaces a newer one (a request number), a failed read clears only what is older, and the sign-out wipe clears the held answer; an answer read under another account is not used. `RadioInfo` across the boundary shrinks from 24 fields to the 5 shared code reads (title, playing, level, speedValue, micLevel). Swift deleted: `BlockClock` and the play-time estimate, `BarTracker.sentences/sentenceIndex/speedName/clockProgress`, `ReaderFollow` (a three-line shell stays only because `AccountWipe.swift` still names it), `ReaderFeed`, `HeldPages.normalised/all/body`, `PlaybackSpeed.name`, and the tests of only those. The hand-over of the page to the voice no longer carries sentences.

Cost of sending the words every pulse: block text is one paragraph. In the fixture corpus (78 paragraphs over 40 characters in `mobile/fixtures`) the median is 126 characters and the longest 8193, so one read a second moves about 0.1 KB typically and 8 KB at worst, with no hashing and no cache to keep coherent.

Why: the phone app is Exact and only voice is native; these were logic (names, sentence splitting, progress arithmetic, matching words to blocks) computed in Swift for shared code to read back. What stays native is plain fact: the microphone level, the speed the player is at, the audio's own position, the document only native holds, the engine's events.

Proof: `bun test` in `mobile/AutoradioLab` (counts in the landing report), `bun exact.mjs contract build app.contract` (warnings only), `bun exact.mjs test web` (only the two kokoro failures), `swift test` of `mobile/AutoradioKit` through the lock, the boundary and entry gates. `radio-follow.test.ts` runs the real path (radio on air, native's answers faked, the reader's held page): lit block and sentence, paused, page not held, wipe and relight, a document replaced mid-play, another entry's words, one native read a pulse. Four of its behaviours were broken on purpose and failed. Not proved: a device build. A device must show the reader lighting the sentence the radio is saying, going dark on pause and on sign-out, and following a reload.

Replaced: the Swift sentence split, sentence index, progress, `ReaderFollow`/`ReaderFeed` and the native `readerFollow` op. The radio's sentences are now cut by the reader's regex splitter, not the platform's, so a split may differ from before at an odd abbreviation.
