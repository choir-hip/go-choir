# RLMs are RLMs: An ontology of persistent inquiry

Independent research brief | September 10, 2026

This is a separate synthesis of the 34-page *RLMs are RLMs* memo, the attached delta note, and the accompanying response to the adversarial review attributed there to DeepSeek 4.1 Flash. It proposes an ontology and a narrower next experiment; it reports no experimental results. The original memo, reviewer claims, and principal's responses remain distinct inputs.

My proposed center is **an inquiry whose interpretations, evidence, and methods of investigation can change while their earlier relationships remain recoverable**. Prospective prediction is an instrument within that process. The learner includes the frozen model, the context-construction policy, and the persistent environment. The interesting result would be that this composite becomes better at assimilating unfamiliar material and carrying its distinctions into writing. Greater forecast accuracy would establish one component of that result.

Three processes need separate names. **Assimilation** changes what the system understands about the present object. **Learning** changes how it approaches subsequent objects because of experience. **Publication** turns some current understanding into an artifact for another person. A corrected interpretation is evidence of assimilation. A later reduction in the same kind of misreading is evidence of learning. A faithful explanation or useful draft tests publication. Keeping these separate prevents a successful forecast, a changed belief, and better writing from silently becoming interchangeable measures.

This has several intellectual ancestors beyond agent memory. Dawid's prequential tradition evaluates procedures through predictions made before successive observations; Gneiting and Raftery connect sequential predictive scores to model comparison. General value functions and the Horde research program treat many predictions about experience under different policies as a representation of knowledge. Assumption-based truth maintenance preserves the assumptions under which conclusions hold. These supply useful components of the ontology, although none establishes the proposed learning mechanism in a frozen language model. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf); [White, Modayil, and Sutton, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.6262); [Reiter and de Kleer, 1987](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1987/AAAI87-033.pdf).

I would organize the durable objects as follows. These are conceptual distinctions; they do not require separate databases or services.

| Object | What it preserves | Boundary that matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inquiry | Question, purpose, scope, intended use, and open unknowns | Purpose determines relevance; confidence does not determine purpose. |
| Interpretation | Versioned hypotheses, assumptions, supporting passages, tensions, and alternatives | An interpretation can overlap another, remain unresolved, or be replaced by a newly discovered possibility. |
| Probe | Proposed query, source read, experiment, or action; selection rationale and cost | The agent chooses a way of obtaining evidence, which influences what it observes. |
| Forecast | Target and class, probability or distribution, horizon, hypothesis and prior-evidence references, and resolution contract | A forecast about a source's contents differs from a forecast about the world. |
| Observation | Retrieved bytes or measured result, source/version, publication and exposure times, and acquisition conditions | Recording a report does not establish the report's truth. |
| Adjudication | Versioned judgment mapping observations to a forecast's target; resolver, evidence, uncertainty, and status | Pending, disputed, and unresolvable are legitimate states. |
| Assessment | Proper score, calibration analysis, or downstream utility estimate, with method and version | A forecast score, a cohort statistic, and a causal estimate are different kinds of evidence. |
| Revision | Change to an interpretation or procedure, with its grounds and scope | Updating this inquiry and learning a reusable method must be distinguishable. |
| Projection | Current working gestalt, StoryGraph view, brief, or proposed procedure | Compression and synthesis remain traceable to the underlying records. |

An **episode** is a connected selection of these objects. One observation can bear on several forecasts; one forecast can acquire several adjudications; one inquiry can remain active through thousands of episodes. This suggests an append-only event history with typed relationships and rebuildable views. It also answers the gestalt question: the gestalt is a compact working projection of the current inquiry, including unresolved tensions and pointers to alternatives. It should have no independent authority to erase them.

Plural hypotheses are the right response to premature assimilation, but plurality alone is insufficient. A model can generate three paraphrases of one assumption, assign them impressive-looking weights, and become more confident in the shared mistake. Hypotheses therefore need explicit relations: incompatible, compatible, conditional, narrower, or differently scoped. Normalize weights only within an explicitly exclusive and exhaustive comparison. For open interpretations, preserve qualitative support and uncertainty rather than forcing everything into a categorical distribution. The architecture must also permit new hypotheses after observation and retain the evidence that the original set was inadequate.

The prospective object should expose a comparison such as `q_i(O | H_i, E_t, a_t)`: what observation each hypothesis predicts, given the evidence already available and the planned probe. Where these are defensible likelihoods, their ratio supplies relative evidence after observation. With language-generated likelihoods, that calculation is a candidate approximation whose reliability needs testing. A hypothesis can predict an observation well because the agent has modeled the source's rhetoric or the search engine's selection behavior. That success need not support the hypothesis's account of reality. The probe and observation channel belong inside the conditioning information.

For example, suppose World Wire is investigating whether a company's retreat reflects financing pressure or an operational delay. Both explanations predict an optimistic press release. Correctly forecasting its language earns a source-content score but barely distinguishes the explanations. A financing filing or a dated operational milestone may distinguish them. Three articles repeating the same announcement provide three exposure events but substantially shared evidence. Record that dependence before treating corroboration counts or accumulated scores as learning signal. Preserve some investigation budget for observations that challenge the whole framing, since requiring every probe to discriminate existing hypotheses could suppress discovery.

The evaluation layer needs more separation than the phrase "scoring predictions" suggests. First, a resolver determines what outcome the evidence supports. Then a scoring rule evaluates the forecast against that outcome. Calibration is assessed over comparable collections of forecasts; a single 70% prediction cannot be declared calibrated or uncalibrated from one resolution. Usefulness asks what the investigation accomplished, relative to its purpose and cost. These operations have different failure modes and should not share a single undifferentiated reward field.

Proper scoring rules provide incentives for honest probability reporting under their assumptions. They do not ensure honest reporting by an arbitrary frozen model, repair a mistaken resolution, or make scores on self-selected questions comparable. The original memo already cites Brier, Good, and Gneiting-Raftery; the bibliography gap is especially the empirical forecasting literature and its evaluation practice. Halawi and colleagues supply a retrieval-and-aggregation baseline, while ForecastBench supplies prospective evaluation on unresolved questions. The Halawi paper's authors are Danny Halawi, Fred Zhang, Chen Yueh-Han, and Jacob Steinhardt; the delta note's attribution including Novikov is incorrect. [Gneiting and Raftery, 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf); [Halawi et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563); [Karger et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19839).

I would soften the proposed calibration precondition. Miscalibrated confidence can remain informative: a model's 80% answers might be more reliable than its 60% answers while both are overconfident. That can support recalibration and useful trace selection. Even uninformative confidence does not erase information in the prediction, observation, and revision. The experimental precondition is a measured elicitation protocol, with its strengths and limitations characterized. Test confidence discrimination, calibration, and sensitivity to relevant evidence separately. Existing work shows that the format and elicitation method matter; it does not certify the model configurations proposed here. [Kadavath et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221); [Tian et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975).

Keep raw elicited probabilities and any calibrated transformation separately, version the transformation, and fit it only on prior data. Otherwise an improved external calibrator can be mistaken for improved model behavior. More generally, learning to anticipate a resolver's judgment differs from learning about the resolver's target. Paleka and colleagues show how label noise can change the incentive to report true world probabilities, alongside problems with temporal leakage and benchmark composition. Their earlier consistency checks provide useful diagnostics before outcomes resolve, but coherent probabilities can still be wrong. [Paleka et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723); [Paleka et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18544).

Usefulness should initially remain a set of downstream observations: whether a probe eliminated a consequential ambiguity, corrected an unsupported claim, changed a retrieval choice, improved a later prediction, or improved the artifact. A changed decision is evidence of influence, not automatically benefit. Expected information gain can eventually help choose probes, but its value depends on the hypothesis space, likelihoods, and question being informative. Even well-specified Bayesian experimental design faces substantial estimation difficulties. An agent can appear to gain information by resolving distinctions it invented that nobody needs. Start with task-grounded comparisons and budgeted exploration; learn a selection proxy after there is evidence that the proxy tracks useful outcomes. [Foster et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05480).

Reading gives this ontology its demanding application. Consider an unfamiliar essay that initially seems to argue that institutions lack intelligence, then distinguishes intellectual capacity from institutional receptivity to ideas. A useful reader retains the earlier interpretation, recognizes the later distinction, and revisits passages whose meaning changes. A system rewarded only for predicting the next conceptual move could favor familiar argumentative templates and penalize surprise. The outcome worth testing is whether its eventual interpretation accommodates the actual text while preserving the author's distinctions, qualifications, and unresolved claims. Failure to anticipate an original move can coexist with excellent reading once that move arrives.

Semantic evaluation should therefore distinguish factual mistakes, unsupported interpretations, legitimate ambiguity, and preference disagreement. Some reading tasks have strong anchors: contradiction with a passage, incorrect attribution, omission of an explicit qualification, or failure on a carefully constructed continuation. Others admit several defensible readings. Plank's work on human label variation directly challenges the assumption that disagreement should always be collapsed into a single gold label. Preserve judgments and rationales by evaluator; reserve adjudicated targets for the questions that support them. A model's agreement with other models is not sufficient evidence of fidelity to an unfamiliar author. [Plank, 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.731/).

This supports the two-track scientific sequence in the notes. Mechanically resolved tasks can establish whether prospective experience helps later behavior. Reading and writing then test whether the mechanism transfers to the motivating application. Keep a small reading evaluation active from the beginning so that engineering choices remain exposed to that purpose. Use both controlled texts, whose critical distinctions are specified independently, and unfamiliar human-authored texts with separately attributed judgments. A test-suite success establishes something about the mechanism; improvement in reading requires its own evidence.

I also agree that the learning horizon should be central, with a sharper definition. A frozen model given identical state does not forget merely because a day has passed. The relevant variables are elapsed world time, intervening activity, context displacement, source change, and difficulty retrieving the earlier state. Vary those separately. A context reset followed by distracting tasks can test an important part of the hypothesis without waiting months. A live stream then adds genuine environmental change and delayed evidence. A null result over ten seconds weakens an immediate-episode benefit; a null result across substantial displacement, relevant repeated opportunities, and usable retrieval weighs much more heavily against persistent adaptation.

The cleanest next experiment separates **recording a commitment** from **conditioning later cognition on it**. Compare equal-budget retrospective reflection with a sealed pre-observation state record, a sealed explicit forecast, and an actively maintained hypothesis portfolio. Sealed records remain unavailable to the first interpretation of the observation and become available afterward for comparison and future learning. This helps distinguish the benefits of preserving a before-state, eliciting a prediction, and keeping predictions cognitively active. Give the retrospective arm an equivalent allowance for reasoning. Begin with matched evidence exposure; test adaptive retrieval separately because it changes which evidence each arm receives.

To establish learning, evaluate subsequent unfamiliar tasks with current evidence held constant. Compare factual history alone, prospective traces without scores, and scored traces under matched context budgets. Shuffled scores provide a further diagnostic if scores appear necessary. History removal and restoration locate the benefit in external state, but do not by themselves distinguish learning from access to additional facts. Report held-out performance, recurrence of specified error classes, retention after displacement, and the full inference cost including elicitation, retrieval, scoring, and maintenance. RLMs provide access beyond the active window, and ICRL research provides a precedent for reward-conditioned adaptation; the conjunction still needs this evidence. [Zhang, Kraska, and Khattab, 2025/2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601); [Song et al., 2025/2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06303).

Provenance is required from the beginning, but causal credit needs an additional step. "This trace was retrieved before the successful answer" records exposure. "This trace improved the answer" requires a comparison, such as withholding it in a controlled replay or randomizing access where feasible. MemQ provides directly relevant machinery for propagating value along memory dependencies, with structural depth taking the place of simple temporal distance. That is a learning rule over recorded dependencies; the graph alone does not identify each memory's causal contribution. Keep exposure, evidential support, and estimated causal influence as different edge types. [Liao et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08374).

The same discipline applies to multiple models. Shared observations, separately authored interpretations, ensemble forecasts, and transferred learning traces are distinct objects and operations. Research on LLM forecast aggregation establishes a useful baseline, but does not establish that one family benefits from another's error history. Record which earlier reviews each reviewer saw: independently noticed criticism and criticism repeated after exposure have different evidential meanings. This brief has seen both the memo and the adversarial response, so it is an additional synthesis rather than a blind replicate. Retain model and configuration identity, evidence exposure, and task-specific results when estimating transfer. [Schoenegger et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379).

For implementation, I would place these records within the computer's existing canonical event authority and expose typed queries through each agent's persistent REPL. That is a recommendation conditional on the delta note's account of Choir; I have not inspected the repository or its governing documents. Multiple model families can participate within one computer without assuming that different users' computers share state. An explicit publication or import event can govern wider trace exchange. A compiled procedure is a versioned artifact derived from selected experience, with its evaluation record still addressable.

The remaining research choice is how much of interpretation can be usefully typed without making the system prematurely impose its own ontology on the material. Type identity, scope, provenance, exposure, prediction targets, and evaluation contracts first. Let the internal content of hypotheses remain comparatively expressive. The next brief should be informed by whether preserving these distinctions produces fewer repeated misreadings after context displacement, and whether the strongest gains come from prospective elicitation, maintained plurality, or simply a recoverable history. Those outcomes would imply meaningfully different architectures.
