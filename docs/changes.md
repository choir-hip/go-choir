### No host log entry on Node B outlives 30 days (`lane/log-retention`, BLACK: it deletes files in production; needs the owner's word)
- **What:** an upper bound, with margin, on every request, service and login log. Caddy's access log goes to the journal instead of files; the journal is limited to 21 days and a job (`go-choir-log-age`, daily and two minutes after every boot) closes its open file, trims it and checks the result, so the worst case is 24 days. Login-attempt records are daily files kept 21 days. The last-login record is removed at every run and sshd no longer prints it. The job also deletes access-log files left from before (by their oldest entry) and three dead operator log files. The disk sweep and the deploy preflight trim the journal by time, not to 256 MB.

[Problem record](problems/host-logs-kept-a-day-or-for-ever-2026-11-24.md): the worst cases, what can still exceed 30 days, the two policy sentences for the owner to choose between, what neither covers, and the exact list of what the first run deletes.

- **To know:** the first run deletes on Node B: 5 access-log files (103 MB, first entries March to May), 3 last-login files, 3 dead operator outputs, and logrotate deletes `btmp.1`; 11 more access-log files go over the next 21 days. Access logs were kept 90 days and are now at most 24; read them with `sudo journalctl -u caddy -o cat`. It is an upper bound only: a nearly full disk may delete sooner. - **Replaced:** `journalctl --vacuum-size=256M` in two places; Caddy's access-log files (100 MB, 10 files, 90 days); NixOS's monthly login-record rotation; earlier versions of this change. **Undo:** revert; deleted files do not come back.
### Intake: a busy desk no longer starves the capture queue until it expires (`lane/intake-starve`, orange)
- **What:** four changes to the management-task queue. (a) A task's run has its own dispatcher lane, as wide as the most task slots (8), so a claimed task never waits behind other desks for one of the twelve shared slots: a held task slot always has a run the dispatcher can start at once. (b) A free slot goes to the oldest queued task, in the starter and in the start a closure mints. (c) The 24-hour queue expiry is removed: no task leaves the queue undecided. (d) Once a minute the runtime logs `runtime: management task queue: queued=N oldest_age_s=S running=R slots=K computer=C`.
- **Why:** on 2026-11-19 and 11-20 the paper closed 481 tasks, 1,352 captures, as `expired_in_queue`, and on 11-23 163 tasks waited up to 13.7 hours. One mechanism: other desks filled the shared dispatcher, each task waited minutes for it while holding a task slot, newest-first starved the old tasks and the expiry closed them a day later with no error. [Problem record](problems/captures-dropped-and-stalled-2026-11-24.md).
- **Proof:** tests written first: `TestTaskRunsStartWhileOtherDesksHoldEverySharedSlot`, `TestKernelDispatcherHasTheTaskLane`, `TestQueuedTasksStartOldestFirst`, `TestAQueuedTaskNeverExpires`, `TestTheQueueIsLoggedOnceAMinute`. **Not proved on the paper**; ledger C248: the queue line's `oldest_age_s` stays under 3,600 through a busy research hour.
- **To know:** up to four more runs may be active at once than before (task runs no longer count against the twelve shared slots). A queue that outruns its slots now grows without bound and says so in the log; nothing trims it. Tasks already older than a day at the deploy will run, oldest first.
- **Replaced:** `store.NewestQueuedFirst`, the expiry sweep (`expireStaleQueuedTasks`) and its tests. The `expired_in_queue` value stays for records already written. **Undo:** revert.
