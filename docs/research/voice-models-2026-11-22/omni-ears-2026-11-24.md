# LFM3-Omni-2B as the ears, measured against Fluid Speech (2026-11-24)

Owner, 2026-11-24: "we should test using omni for speech in speech out. in place of our speech pipeline... what is its perplexity on noisy speech? if it has strong ears then it's essential." "Perplexity" is read as how well it hears: word error. The summary and the phone question are in `docs/reference/ON-DEVICE-MODELS.md` section 14; this file holds the tables. Mac (M-series), not a phone. Every number below is **ours** (run 2026-11-24).

## Method

- Omni: `LiquidAI/LFM3-Omni-2B-GGUF` at commit `d508b88f`, Q8_0 and `mmproj-...-Q8_0` (Q4_0 pairs where marked), llama.cpp b12259 (the app's pinned tag; the macOS arm64 release binaries), `llama serve`, one `/v1/chat/completions` request per clip, the wav as `input_audio`, system prompt `Perform ASR in English.` (the card's), greedy, no user text. The model hears the whole clip; no VAD, no chunking.
- Fluid Speech Pro, Mini and Parakeet TDT v2: the app's own stages through `FluidSpeechProTests/testTranscribeSetsToFiles` (added here; FluidAudio 0.18.3, one model per process), the same wavs.
- Scoring: `scripts/omni-ears/wer_table.py`, the harness's normalisation (lower-case, "harbour" to "harbor", split on every non-alphanumeric; word edit distance over reference length; corpus rate). So "Mr." against "mister" and "12" against "twelve" count as errors for every recogniser alike (the Fluid models write punctuated, cased text too).
- Sets (`scripts/omni-ears/build_sets.py`): LibriSpeech clean (the earlier builder's 73 clips, 1,169 words); LibriSpeech other validation, first 100 clips (1,828 words, the earlier set's count); AMI single distant microphone test, first 47 clips of four words or more (**508 words, 2.3 min: not the earlier builder's 581-word set**, whose selection is not recorded; compare the recognisers here with each other, not with the earlier table).
- Noise: DEMAND PCAFETER (cafeteria, with babble) and STRAFFIC (street), channel 01, Zenodo 1227121, CC BY 4.0, mixed into the 73 clean clips at 10, 5 and 0 dB signal-to-noise (noise gain from RMS over the clip; a random stretch of the noise; fixed seed).
- The radio's voice bed: **a second LibriSpeech speaker** (validation.clean speakers 1462, 1673, 174, 1919, 1988, 1993, none in the clean set; 40 clips each joined into a stream), band-limited 300 Hz to 6 kHz as a phone speaker would, mixed in at 10, 5 and 0 dB. A stand-in: the real radio is a synthesised voice from a phone speaker into the phone's own microphone, which adds room and speaker distortion not modelled here. No echo cancellation in front of any recogniser.
- No speech: 16 clips with an empty reference: 5 digital silences (2 to 12 s), 3 room-tone clips, 4 cafeteria and 4 street noise clips (3 to 10 s). Every word written is invented.

The kernel footprint ledger does not count mapped weights (llama.cpp maps the file; Core ML's Neural Engine weights are also outside it), so resident size is the figure to read for Omni. Omni is about nine times slower than Fluid per second of audio on this Mac, on the GPU against the Neural Engine.

Long audio: a 110 s clip came back whole (257 of 259 words, Q4_0); a 188 s clip looped and wrote 1,229 words for a 453-word reference. The LiteRT build reads audio in 30 s windows (its card).

## Understanding without transcription (hand read by the author of this work, not independent)

20 spoken questions (macOS `say`, four voices), the audio alone as the user turn, system prompt `Respond with text.`; answers read against the expected one (`ask_omni.py`). Control: the same 20 typed.

| Condition | Correct (of 20) | Wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| typed text (control) | 20 | none |
| spoken, clean | 19 | racket and shuttlecock: "Golf." |
| spoken, 5 dB radio bed | 17 | closest planet: "Mars"; half of one hundred: an unrelated answer about "the 1100", a computer; shuttlecock: "Golf." |

So it answers a question from audio, and at 5 dB it loses one to three of twenty. These are synthesised voices; a person's voice is harder.

## Speech out

Works on llama.cpp b12259 two ways: `llama-tts -m <gguf> -mm <mmproj> -p <text>` (14.4 frames per second of 80 ms, 1.24 times real time; written when done) and `llama serve`'s `/v1/realtime` WebSocket (streamed `response.output_audio.delta`, pcm16 24 kHz; `scripts/omni-ears/s2s_realtime.py`).
- Text read aloud (system "Perform TTS in American English using a female voice."): first audio 0.25 to 0.33 s after `response.create`; 8 to 9 s of speech in 5.8 to 7.9 s. Played back through Omni's own ASR the five two-sentence transitions come back word for word but for one spelling (queueing, queuing).
- Spoken question, spoken answer: the session must ask for `output_modalities: ["text","audio"]` (the card's "interleaved"). With `["audio"]` alone the speech is not an answer (the first five samples decoded as "Quarries.", "Yeah." and the like). With both, the five answers are right and spoken, first audio 0.71 to 1.24 s after the question is committed, 4.6 to 9.8 s in all for 4 to 11 s of speech, each ending with a stock follow-up ("Would you like to know anything else about it?").
- Ten samples with prompt and timings: `/Volumes/ChoirArchive/evidence/omni-voice-2026-11-24/` (not in the repository). I did not listen to them; the check is the ASR read-back above, not an ear.

## Tool calling, text in and text out (a probe, not a build)

Three tools as JSON schema (`reorder_queue(item_ids)`, `skip(item_id)`, `say_transition(text, before_item_id)`), a queue of six items in the system prompt, 20 listener requests (`scripts/omni-ears/probe_tools.py`); the model's own template renders the tools and it writes `<tool_call><function=name><parameter=k>v</parameter></function></tool_call>`. Greedy, Q8_0 both; the scorer is automatic, my expectations are in the script.

| | LFM3-Omni-2B | LFM3-1.7B (Q8_0; the app's responder is the QAD Q4_0 of the same model) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right (tool and arguments, or correctly no call), of 20 | 14 | 13 |
| Of the 13 requests that need a call: right tool / right arguments | 11 / 8 | 9 / 6 |
| Of the 7 that need no call: no call | 6 | 7 |
| Invented ids | 0 | 0 |
| Decode speed (Mac) | 26 tokens per second | 23 tokens per second |

Omni's misses: `say_transition` for "play the Mars one next" and "play the science one" (should reorder); for the two transition requests the right tool but always `before_item_id` of the playing item; `skip` on "the story about the election" (not in the queue); an unchanged queue for "football report before the jazz". LFM3-1.7B often wrote "Skipping the current item." in words and made no call, and reordered with partial id lists; for the absent story it said "Skipping that item now." with no call. 20 prompts is a probe: a one-prompt difference is noise.

Code: asked three times for `runLengthEncode` in at most ten lines, Omni wrote the same 15-line function each time with the count before the character ("3a1b2c") where "a3b1c2" was asked, so all three fail the check run with `bun`.
