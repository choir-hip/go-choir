## (1) The frame I reject

The brief and the doc share one unstated organizing verb: **placement**. §0: "sites ... advertise what they have ... Work is placed on sites by those attributes." Underneath that verb sit five load-bearing assumptions nobody is arguing against:

1. **The site is the primitive.** Everything (trust class, capacity, admission, rings) hangs off a fixed machine with a signed site record. Work is a guest at the machine.
2. **The writer is a leaseholder.** "One writer per branch, always a host" assumes a contention-fight model: two parties could want the role, so someone must fence. `writer_handoff`, automatic failover branches, "lease has no expiry" (§10.3) are all tenancy law, not mechanism.
3. **The home is a live relay.** §0.2: "A phone always reaches a computer through its home ... The home relays live traffic." Reachability is equated with one server being awake and in the path.
4. **Phones are second-class.** "Phones and browsers are clients, not sites" — no state to protect, onlyctors to consume.
5. **Safety of the configuration space is an admission problem.** Q9 asks for a kernel plus model checking so "every configuration is safe"; the machinery implied is a central gate list plus a checker over the composition.

## (2) The reframe: this is not a resource fabric, it's a jurisdiction system

Invert the subject. **The work unit is the bearer of credentials; sites are venues that recognize or refuse credentials.** Nothing plays "sites advertise, work gets placed"; instead, a unit of work travels under a signed, attenuatable, revocable credential and settles wherever recognition exists. Placement becomes recognition. Concretely, three imported structures from distant domains:

- **Diplomatic law → every actor is an envoy, not a tenant.** An ambassador is *accredited* to a specific court by letter of credence: verified on arrival, revocable at will, expiring by term, and revocable with "persona non grata" with no appeal. The ambassador has no lease on the embassy — tenure continues only while the credential is in force. Map: *writer realization = ambassador*; *writer lease = letter of credence*, not a fencing token (kills Q1's "do leases expire —” yes, credentials are defined by term and revocability, not TTL liveness); *org denies A's VM on B's workstation = cabotage law* — domestic shipping reserved to domestic carriers, enforced at the port by every jurisdiction, not by a central scheduler (Q3, Q4).
- **The immune system → enforcement is local, not central.** Tissue isn't guarded by a gate list at the perimeter; any cell that doesn't present the right surface marker gets destroyed by *any neighbor that inspects it*, before it can attach. If the fabric's invariant is "no bearer attaches anywhere without a valid credential chain, validated at every attachment point," then you never have to model-check the composition of org policies (Q9's exponential blowup) — you model-check the *handshake* once, and let policies intercept the zeros: default-deny is monotone, so two bad policies compose to "refused", never to "escalated". Destructive composition requires an affirmative grant at both ends, which is checkable locally.
- **Maritime flags of convenience → the failure you must design against, not adopt.** Open registries already exist in compute: running an org's work on an employee's personally-owned Mac is flying a Panama flag. The known history is that flag-of-convenience regimes fail on exactly the three axes in this brief — audit, liability, recall. So the design constraint is not "don't do it" but "the flag (site record) can never be more permissive than the cargo's credential", which is a one-line validator rule, not a policy debate.

Under this frame the rest of the brief rewrites itself:

- **Q2 (reachability):** the home is demoted from live relay to **registered agent / process-service address** — corporate law already solved this: the registered office is where process is served and records are kept, not where the work happens. The home's real job: (a) hold a certifiable current-address record (route, updated by the writer's outbound session), (b) serve last-durable read-only state as archive, (c) notarize the writer's letter of credence. Phone-to-writer traffic punches direct after rendezvous via the home; only rendezvous and fallback traverse the home. "Multiple homes" stops being a routing nightmare and becomes "multiple registered addresses are fine; only one may claim service-of-process continuity" — a bookkeeping rule, not a mechanism.
- **Q4 (placement):** becomes navigation down a recognition gradient: work moves toward sites that recognize more of its credential for lower cost. "Admission" is per-site validation of the credential against its local jurisdiction map, not a placement algorithm run by a management desk.
- **Phones as highest-privilege sites, not clients:** the phone is the only site that is also the user's body — microphone, screen time, biometric channel, on-device models. In biology terms the phone is the **somatic stratum**: reflexes (voice capture, computer-use pointing, read-aloud) run locally with blanket fast-path authorization, and only deliberation crosses the fabric into approval territory. Currently the design would reflex-cap the fabric (seconds of round-trip to the home) and devalue the most intimate surface it has.
- **Q5 (irreversible effects):** under the jurisdiction frame, the outbox item is a **docketed legal instrument, not a queue message**. It exists in three legally distinguishable states — *docketed (notarized precommitment) / performed / performed-unacked* — with the home as the countersigning notary for "docketed" and receipt-through-notary for "performed". That's a semantics change with practical teeth: unacked-performed is a resolvable status requiring an *opposite-kind* receipt ("observed sent"), not a re-queue. Exactly-once stops being a distributed-systems slogan and becomes an audit question with the tape as the docket book.
- **Q6 (Choir in Choir):** one credential theareot is already implicitly in the doc — "addressed publication, never a parent's write" — but in this frame it's not an exception, it's the default: a controller computer carries an envoy credential *to* the child, and the child's writer (as venue) refuses every action not on it.
- **Q8 (org lifecycle):** offboarding is credential recall, not property seizure. §10.9 already says the employee's computer goes with the employee; the reframe fills the gap: revocation is nunc pro tunc — the credential is dead going forward and the org's published graph is retained because the graph recipients hold *receiving* credentials, not continuing-upload credentials.
- **Q10 (self-tuning inside bounds):** self-modification is credential *renegotiation* with the same validator; the bounds are the visualizer's "banner you can't sign yourself" — an envoy cannot extend its own letters.
- **Management desk:** shrinks from decision-maker to **registrar**. Notaries don't decide who gets married; they attest who did. Grants are authored by the requester, rolled into signed credentials, and the management desk's only unique authority is issuing and revoking keys in the registry — which is Q3's primitive, not Q1's.

## (3) What changes in practice

- The kernel's job list collapses: delete "placement algorithm and admission" from the design's must-haves; what's left is (a) credential issuance/attenuation/revocation and (b) a validator contract every site implements. That's a much smaller kernel — closer to "prove the handshake once" than "prove the policy space".
- §10.1's owner-accepted "placement by trust, owned by the management desk" gets rescoped: management owns the registry, not the placement. That is a real change to an accepted proposal, so it needs the owner — flag explicitly.
- The home's SLA drops from "always-reachable relay" to "reachable when process must be served and when doing notary work" — availability targets get an order of magnitude looser, and cross-region latency stops being a first-order design input.
- Q1 leases and letter-of-credence terms unify: no unconditional expiry (matches §10.3), but the credential itself carries its term and revocability; automatic host failover for standing intents becomes "envoy arrest" — the country's duties run with a caretaker, not a second ambassador.
- Phones graduate from client to body-class site with separate reflex fast-path — a new mechanism, worth flagging as the odd one out.
- Verification strategy for Q9 changes shape: stop enumerating policy transitions; enumerate handshake invariants (credential chain present at every attach, revocation is nunc pro tunc, term bounded, one service-of-process home) and model-check those, not the composition.

## (4) The sharpest objection to my own reframe

**Credentials solve refusal; they do not solve continuity.** Capability/diplomatic-accreditation is a *pointwise* pattern: each attach-site checks the bearer against a policy it holds. But Q1/Q2 are *global sequence* properties: the tape's head must keep advancing from exactly one realization while that realization's venue sleeps, dies, or partitions — and the last thing a credential system tells you, when the ambassador is unreachable and the term is unsettled, is nothing. A tissue cell that fails self-recognition gets destroyed; nobody in the immune system replaces the ambassador'solo flies the standing-intent schedule. The current writer/home/lease frame — a named fiduciary plus a registered-address notary — is the historical winner for this class of problem precisely because credentials are the wrong tool for presence (diplomatic precedent: empires hash out recall crises over embassies, not visite cards).

Honest version of my reframe, then: it is **strictly better for Q3/Q4/Q5/Q7/Q8** (attenuatable trust, local validators, docketed instruments) and **strictly worse for Q1/Q2** than what's in the doc, and the question for the panel is which of the two patterns these ten questions actually decompose into — my bet is that they cleanly split into a *consensus spine* (Q1, Q2: fiduciary + registered agent, current frame mostly right) and a *credential web* (Q3–Q8, Q10: everything else), and that the fabric is over-designed assuming the consensus spine's machinery has to be the same substrate the credential web runs on. That split — not one kernel, two small ones taped by one registry — is the concrete alternative the first-principles pass should test against a single-kernel design.
