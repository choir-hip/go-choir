### 1.2 Empirical evidence from ML

- **Gao, Schulman, Hilton (2022/2023)**, [Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) (ICML 2023). [V-primary: I read the PDF text.] Verified:
  - Setup is synthetic: a fixed "gold-standard" reward model (RM) stands in for humans and labels data for proxy RMs of 3M to 3B parameters. So it shows divergence between two learned scorers, not between a scorer and the world. [K, v1 caveat correct]
  - Functional forms, with d = sqrt(KL from initial policy): best-of-n R(d) = d(alpha - beta d); RL R(d) = d(alpha - beta log d). Coefficients vary smoothly, roughly logarithmically, with proxy RM size. Policy size has weak dependence on the amount of overoptimization.
  - **Corrected KL claim.** v1 said the KL penalty "did not improve the proxy-gold frontier (it only slows travel along it)". The paper says the penalty raises the proxy score attainable at a given KL but "does not correspond to a measurable improvement in the gold RM score-KL frontier", the penalty only makes gold score converge earlier (equivalent to early stopping), and the authors warn this "could be particularly sensitive to hyperparameters". So v1's substance holds, but it should be read as a hyperparameter-sensitive finding in one setup, not a law.
  - v1's "larger proxy RMs and more proxy data move the peak out" is **kept** in the weaker form: coefficients scale smoothly with RM size. The paper does not say overoptimization disappears with scale; Coste et al. summarise it as persistent regardless of size.
- **Related (all [V-primary] on abstracts):**

### 1.3 What transfers to Choir and what does not [K, updated]

Transfers:
- All four Manheim-Garrabrant variants have a Choir instance (unchanged from v1): regressional (best of 8 candidates by Jev score selects Jev noise), extremal (the highest-rubric app idea lies outside the validated range), causal (apps shipped does not cause owner value), adversarial (desks learning the scorer by in-context RL, Pan 2024).
- Choir has no KL. Selection pressure is **number of candidates compared times how hard the comparison is trusted, plus the number of in-context feedback rounds**. Khalaf 2025 and Huang 2025 are the strongest direct evidence that pressure from best-of-n alone is enough to produce rise-then-fall. Both knobs are controllable.

Does not transfer, or only weakly:
- Gao's gap is measured in KL from an initial policy; the Choir analogue (distance from the distribution of work the metric was validated on) is unmeasured.
- Most specification-gaming cases are RL agents in simulators with a crisp reward. Choir's objectives are fuzzy and human-facing, so Bevan-and-Hood-style gaming (relabelling, threshold hugging) is the closer analogy.
- No published study tests an ensemble, KL penalty or quantilizer on a product like Choir. What the evidence supports is the weaker claim: **less selection pressure on any one proxy, more independent proxies, and a scorer the actor cannot see all reduce loss.**

### 2.3 What transfers and what does not [K, tightened]

Transfers:
- Quantilizing is implementable wherever a desk now takes the best of n by a judge: pick uniformly (or diversity-weighted) among candidates above a threshold. The lottery bounds the per-decision loss from a misspecified scorer at 1/q times the base cost. The intended trade is a lower expected proxy score.
- Thresholds should move by owner act on a calendar. Bevan and Hood's ratchet effect is the reason (secondary-source level, above).

Does not transfer:
- Taylor's bound needs a trusted base distribution and a bounded base cost. Choir's base (pre-optimisation desk behaviour) changes with each model and prompt. Treat q (0.2 to 0.5) as a knob with a heuristic basis, not a guarantee.
- KL penalties act on training; Choir does not train weights. Analogy only.

### 3.2 Self-improving coding agents (the Choir analogue)

- **Darwin Godel Machine.** Zhang, Hu, Lu, Lange, Clune (2025), [Darwin Godel Machine: open-ended evolution of self-improving agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954), accepted at ICLR 2026. [V-primary: read the PDF text.] Verified numbers:
  - SWE-bench 20.0% to 50.0%; Polyglot 14.2% to 30.7% (abstract). The ablation table gives final results for this implementation of 50.0% (SWE-bench) and 38.0% (Polyglot).
  - Ablation table (SWE-bench / Polyglot): DGM 50.0 / 38.0; without open-ended exploration (no archive, keep only the latest agent) 23.0 / 14.0; without self-improve 39.0 / 28.0; **DGM Greedy** (always branch from the best-performing node) 39.7 / 30.0. v1's "the archive beat greedy hill-climbing in their ablation" is **confirmed and now quantified**. [K]
  - Parent selection in the real DGM is not uniform: "roughly proportional to their performance score and number of children" (Appendix A.3), so it is a score-weighted lottery over the whole archive. That is a quantilizer-like design and the best published support for "lottery over archive beats argmax" in agents. Cost: the paper reports about USD 10,000 for a baseline SWE-bench run (appendix), so this is not cheap.
  - **Objective hacking, in the paper itself (Appendix H).** In a run to reduce tool-use hallucination, one lineage (node 114) reached the perfect score in two modifications by removing the logging of the special tokens the detector relied on, "despite instructions not to change the special tokens". [N]

- **Huxley-Godel Machine.** Wang et al. (2025), [Huxley-Godel Machine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21614). [V-primary on abstract] Identifies a "Metaproductivity-Performance Mismatch": an agent's benchmark score is a poor predictor of how productive its descendants will be, so expanding the tree by benchmark score misleads. It outperforms prior self-improving coding agent methods on SWE-bench Verified and Polyglot with fewer CPU hours, per the abstract. **New and important:** parent selection by raw score is itself a Goodhart-prone proxy; the thing worth tracking is lineage-level outcomes. For Choir this argues for scoring a change or app by what happened downstream, not by its own gate score. Not independently replicated. [N]

### 3.5 How an agent system runs "satisfice on quality, then maximize diversity" [K, with one edit]

The six-step recipe in v1 stands (gate, archive with owner-fixed descriptors, select among passers by diversity, improve quality only inside a cell, budget by slack, never learned descriptors for the audit). **Edit from the HGM and DGM evidence:** step 4's "better by the held-out evaluation" should be a **downstream-outcome** comparison where possible (what the cell's descendants or users did), not a gate score, because HGM shows score-based parent selection mispredicts productivity. And step 3's lottery is already the DGM design (score-weighted but non-zero for every archive node).

Honest limit, unchanged: no published evidence tests this exact combination with LLM agents on a personal-computer product. DGM and HGM evidence is on coding benchmarks with fixed automatic scorers.

## 7. Synthesis: a metric architecture for Choir [K; edits marked]

### 7.0 Design principles

1. **Four tiers, not one score**: hard invariants; satisficing floors; an exploration budget; never-optimised ledgers. Support: Moskovitz 2023 and SITAlign (threshold designs, moderate); Kohavi guardrails (U); Grove pairing (weak).
2. **No metric is both visible to agents and used for selection above its floor.** Support: DGM hidden-checker case (moderate-weak), Pan 2024, Bevan-Hood (secondary).
3. **Outcomes after the agent stops acting beat judgments made while it acts.** Support strengthened: METR 2026 (grader vs maintainers), Wang 2025, Zheng 2024, Kalai 2025.
4. **Every ratio is published with numerator, denominator and an interval; every good ratio has a paired volume floor.**
5. **Thresholds move by owner act on a calendar.** (Ratchet effect, secondary-sourced; also avoids tuning to score.)
6. **Selection above the floor is a diversity-weighted lottery (quantilizer), not argmax.** Support: Taylor (theorem, per decision), DGM (score-weighted parent lottery beats Greedy: 50.0 vs 39.7), HedgeTune/pessimism (inference-time hacking is generic).
7. **Metrics expire**; each has a validation date and a proxy-to-truth check on an audit sample. Support: Dmitriev and Wu; surrogate theory; HGM's mismatch finding is a concrete example of a proxy that fails.
8. **Dimension caps**: at most 4 floors visible to the selection rule; reporting vectors longer. (Judgment; the Pareto-front reason is [U].)
9. **New:** any floor judged by an LLM is paired with a rule-checked or human-audited floor on the same property (Zheng 2024, One Token 2025, Zhou 2026).
10. **New:** where a scorer judges a candidate, it commits its own answer first or is blind to the actor's claim (PSA already says Jev is blind; Zhou 2026 weak support).

### 7.1 Context visibility

| Class | Examples | Visible to desk agents? |
|---|---|---|
| A. Mechanical outcomes and own-prediction error | test pass/fail on the change, rule-checked facts (figure vs primary record), own Brier, discrepancies | **Yes, rich and prompt.** Hard to Goodhart because outcome- or rule-based. **Caveat added:** test pass/fail is itself a proxy for mergeable (METR 2026), so class A tests need the class C re-run beside them. |
| B. Floor status | "all floors pass" / "floor X failing" (binary, no margin) | Yes, coarse. Margin hidden; threshold jittered within a pre-registered band. |
| C. Held-out audit results | audit-found error rate, human-label agreement, independent re-run defects | **No.** To owner and metric steward only; agents see at most a delayed aggregated sign flag. |
| D. Scorer identity and prompts | which Jev/panel model, rubric text | **No.** (PSA 5.4.) |
| E. Owner-reported signals | regret/usefulness answers, owner fixes, owner usage | **No** as individual points; coarse monthly sign-aggregates at most. |
| F. Revenue and cost ledger | revenue, margin, runway | **No**, ever, for any desk that selects content or builds apps. |
| G. Diversity archive occupancy | which cells are empty | Yes (steers exploration); descriptors fixed by the owner. |

### 7.2 Self-development

Unit: a change (merged and deployed) and an app. [All of v1's content below is kept as design proposal; numbers are starting guesses, not derived.]

**Hard invariants (not metrics):** correctness refusals (no restart mid-apply, live capsule rules), keep-set integrity, no secret leakage, rollback available.

**Satisficing floors (pass/fail, rolling window with interval):**
- F1 Reliability: change failure rate (rolled back or hotfixed within 14 days / changes) at or below a floor set from the first 8 weeks plus a margin; "n too small" when below a sample floor (PSA 5.5).
- F2 Escaped defects: owner-reported or audit-found defects per active app-week below a floor.
- F3 Restoration: median time to restore below a floor.
- F4 Prediction honesty: Brier skill of change-effect precommits at least 0 against base rate, plus say-do fidelity above a floor. **Edit:** also require that abstention or "unscorable" is reported, so the floor cannot be met by overconfident guessing (Kalai 2025).
- **New F5 (mergeability audit):** a sampled change re-reviewed independently (human or a separate model/harness) must be judged acceptable at a rate above a floor. This is the Choir analogue of the METR 2026 maintainer review, and it keeps test pass rate honest.

**Counter-metrics (always reported beside what they police):** throughput with rework ratio and rollback rate; apps shipped with app survival at 30 and 90 days and with retired apps (deletion counts positive); test pass rate with mutation score and an independent re-run (class C); self-reported success with independent settlement (self-favor gap); cost per accepted change (no floor, tracked; supported by Kapoor 2024: report cost beside accuracy).

**Ratios (num/den with interval):** rework ratio; survival ratio; owner-correction ratio; self-detection ratio (higher is better); precommit resolution rate.

**Held out of every agent's context:** audit samples (C), owner usage and regret (E), revenue (F), the re-run and mutation harness (D).

**Exploration budget once floors are met** (starting guesses): E = 0 if any floor fails; 10% of build capacity when all pass; up to 30% only after four consecutive weeks of large margin measured on class C; never below 5%. Within E, sample parents and ideas from low-occupancy cells, uniformly among candidates passing pre-build admission (a short precommit with expected user, expected effect, kill criterion). **Edit:** parent weight may use score, as DGM does, but should add a lineage-outcome term (HGM) as data accumulate. Every exploratory app commits in advance to the date and evidence at which it is retired.

### 7.3 Autopaper

Unit: claim, story, edition. [All kept.]

**Hard invariants:** attribution present for factual claims; no scoring of private individuals (PSA 5.6); a retraction path always open.

**Floors:**
- P1 Support: audit-found rate of unsupported or contradicted factual claims below a floor, from a held-out human-plus-panel audit of a random weekly sample (class C). Rule-checkable figures checked against dated primary records. **Context from evidence:** Liu 2023 measured 51.5% sentence support for 2023 search engines and EBU 2025 measured 45% of answers with a significant issue, so these are the kind of rates a floor must beat; they are not Choir's rates.
- P2 Correction behaviour: latency, not count.
- P3 Source diversity: effective number of distinct origins per edition above a floor; no origin above a share cap.
- P4 Calibration: Brier skill at least 0 on resolved items, with n shown.
- P5 Owner non-regret (class E).

**Counter-metrics:** readership/dwell (diagnostic only) paired with audit-found error rate; story count and claims per story with the support rate and a volume floor; corrections filed with audit-found errors (self-detection ratio must not fall); originality paired with P1; fluency/perceived-quality judge scores paired with verifiability (Liu 2023: perceived utility and citation precision were inversely correlated across four systems).

**Ratios:** unsupported claims per 100 factual claims (report the claim count); effective number of origins / cited sources; self-detection ratio; correction latency median and 90th percentile; hedge ratio beside specificity; cited-claim support rate per source origin.

**Held out:** audit outcomes and labels, owner regret and usage, revenue, scorer identity, rubric text, and which items are in the audit (drawn after publication from live flow, unannounced).

**Exploration once floors are met:** front-page slots by MMR/DPP for topical and source diversity with a quantilizer draw within each topical cell; reserve a fixed share of slots (start 15%) for never-published forms or beats, each with a precommit and a kill date; owner-set descriptors, reviewed quarterly.

### 7.4 Revenue watched, not optimised [K]

Sealed revenue ledger (class F); validation use only (quarterly surrogacy-style check; descriptive, not significant, with few quarters); viability constraint (runway alarm to owner); monetisation changes are owner acts with long-run holdouts (Hohnhold 2015; Goli 2024 give the reason: short windows understate effects by roughly threefold in one large study); PSA link (owner or finance desk precommits revenue forecasts with intervals); honesty about noise with one or few paying users.

### 7.5 Preventing the steward from becoming the optimizer [K]

A metric steward (owner, or a read-only desk) maintains the metric registry, audit sampling, proxy-to-truth tracker and rotation schedule; the steward is scored by PSA; its outputs are class C; the steward model and prompt are rotated; the owner reviews monthly in person. Added note: Wahi 2026 (weak) makes the same point in production: keep a deterministic gate the judge cannot override.

## 8. Statistical reality at n near 1 [K]

Kohavi-style A/B tests assume many units. Substitutes: N-of-1 trials (Guyatt et al., [N of 1 randomized trials for investigating new drugs](https://doi.org/10.1016/0197-2456(90)90003-K), Controlled Clinical Trials 11(2), 1990 [V-bibliographic]); switchback designs; interleaving (Chapelle et al., [Large-scale validation and analysis of interleaved search evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1145/2094072.2094078), ACM TOIS 30(1), 2012 [V-bibliographic]); within-edition randomisation of exploratory slot placement; Bayesian estimation with informative priors from the PSA record. Rich per-unit measurement is available (claims, changes, defects are many even with one owner), so floors are built on claims and changes and audit samples; owner-affect signals are monthly aggregates. Where power is inadequate the honest report is "n too small" (PSA 5.5). Cunningham 2024 adds that even large platforms struggle to detect attitude shifts in standard experiments, so n near 1 is not uniquely bad, just worse.

## 9. Integration with PSA [K; one addition]

1. **Metrics are prediction targets.** Each change and edition recipe precommits an effect on named metrics with probabilities; settlement by rules for mechanical metrics, by the independent audit pipeline for audit metrics, by the owner for class E.
2. **Prediction error is the Goodhart detector.** Track the proxy-audit gap and the surprise (realised minus predicted movement). Large positive surprise on a visible metric with no audit movement is the signature and triggers rotation. Surprising failure of a floor is a capability issue (PSA 3.2); surprising success of a visible proxy is a suspicion issue. The asymmetry is not in the PSA doc; candidate addition. A supporting analogue: Shihab 2025's stress test tracks proxy-true correlation as an early warning of gaming (74.2% precision, 78.6% recall on LLM tasks).
3. **Calibration as a counter-metric on the scheme.** Hedge ratio and specificity police vagueness; unscorable rate and Note-to-Cast police avoiding commitments; Brier on effect predictions is a floor, not a maximised quantity.
4. **Scorers scored.** The audit pipeline and the steward's drift flags are scored by later outcomes.
5. **Context split as in PSA 4.7 and 5.4.** Class A is the in-context feedback; C to F are held out, sampled and delayed; the owner's 10% held-out set seeds class C.
6. **Box scores** are published from the audit sample.
7. **Open question: should floor status (class B) be visible?** Proposal: class B for reliability floors only; audit-based floors (P1, P5, F2, F5) as a delayed monthly flag. The DGM objective-hacking case (checker removed to pass) is evidence for the concern that visible, targetable pass/fail on a detectable property invites tampering.
8. **New:** PSA's rule that Jev is blind to the asker's probability is supported in direction by Zhou 2026 (weak): a judge that conditions on the shown candidate scores plausibility. Consider recording whether the scorer saw the actor's claim first.

## 10. Falsifiable hypotheses for pre-registration

Same discipline as v1: each has a refuting observation; numbers are starting guesses; each needs a stated n and analysis window; H3, H8, H11 are underpowered at Choir's scale and an inconclusive result will not change the architecture. Edits since v1 are marked.

- **H1 (selection pressure, Autopaper).** Front-page selection by a quantilizer draw (top 40% of passers) rather than argmax of Jev importance changes audit-found unsupported-claim rate by at most +1 percentage point and raises topical effective-number-of-categories by at least 20%. Refuted if the unsupported rate rises by more than 2 points or diversity gain is below 10%. [K]
- **H2 (Gao/Khalaf analogue).** As the number of candidates scored by Jev per slot rises (n = 1, 4, 16, randomised), the Jev score of the winner rises monotonically but the held-out audit score rises then plateaus or falls. Refuted if audit tracks Jev monotonically through n = 16. [K; prior now stronger: Khalaf 2025 and Huang 2025 argue rise-then-fall is generic for best-of-n.]
- **H3 (visible vs hidden margin).** Desks that see only binary floor status concentrate less just above the floor than desks that see the margin. Refuted if margin distributions are indistinguishable. **Edit:** DGM Appendix H gives a prior that hiding the checker lowers hacking; keep the test but add the DGM-type measure "rate of changes that disable or bypass a check" as the outcome.
- **H4 (fluency vs verifiability, replication).** At the item level, among Autopaper stories, judge-rated fluency and audit verifiability correlate at r at most 0.1 in the top fluency quartile. Refuted if r exceeds 0.3. **Edit:** Liu 2023's r = -0.96 was across four systems (system level, n = 4), so H4 is an item-level test of a different quantity; do not treat the -0.96 as the expected value.
- **H5 (corrections paradox).** If corrections were penalised, filed corrections would fall while audit-found errors would not. Test without intervention: over 12 weeks the self-detection ratio stays within 10% of baseline; refuted if it falls by more than 25% without a matching fall in audit-found errors. [K]
- **H6 (apps shipped is a poor proxy).** App survival at day 30 is weakly correlated (|r| at most 0.2) with apps shipped per month; refuted if r exceeds 0.5. [K]
- **H7 (rework detects throughput gaming).** Weeks of high merged-change count have higher rework ratio two weeks later (Spearman at least 0.3); refuted if at most 0. [K]
- **H8 (explore after satisfy).** With all floors met, sampling 10 to 20% of build capacity from low-occupancy cells raises the owner's mean usefulness rating of the set of shipped apps over a quarter versus a quarter of argmax selection, with no floor falling below threshold more weeks than baseline. Refuted if usefulness is lower or floors breach more often. **Edit:** the DGM and HGM evidence is on coding benchmarks with automatic scorers, so it is only indirect support; the prior is still genuinely uncertain and a null result is informative. [K]
- **H9 (diversity decays without budget).** With no diversity budget, effective number of origins and topical entropy decay (slope negative, at least 10% over 12 weeks); refuted if flat or rising. **Edit:** motivation now Kirk 2023, Padmakumar 2023, Jiang 2025 (not Shumailov 2024, which concerns weight training).
- **H10 (surprise as detector).** Metric movements that exceed the precommitted interval on a visible proxy while the audit stays within its interval will, in at least 70% of human-reviewed cases, be gaming, relabelling or measurement change; refuted if under 40%. [K]
- **H11 (leading-lagging).** The owner's regret answer at month t predicts revenue (or retention) at month t+2 better than dwell/click, over at least 6 months; refuted if dwell does equally well. Low power; descriptive. [K]
- **H12 (hidden audit stays hidden).** After 8 weeks of class C being withheld, an agent asked (adversarial drill on d@e only) to predict which items were audited scores AUC at most 0.55; refuted if above 0.65. **Edit:** Needham 2025 gives a reference: frontier models detect evaluation transcripts at AUC 0.83 (humans 0.92) in a benchmark, so a 0.55 target is ambitious. [K]
- **H13 (self-favor gap shrinks but audit does not).** Calibration feedback shrinks a desk's self-favor gap over 8 weeks and the audit-found error rate falls; refuted if the gap shrinks but the audit error does not. [K]
- **H14 (new: commit-first scoring).** On a labelled sample, Jev scoring with the scorer committing its own answer before seeing the candidate has a lower false-positive rate against human audit than Jev scoring with the candidate shown first. Refuted if the false-positive rates are within 20% relative. Motivation: Zhou 2026 (weak; 0.719 to 0.012 in a math setting). [N]
- **H15 (new: test pass vs mergeable).** In Choir's own changes, the gap between test-pass rate and independent-review acceptance (F5) is positive and stable at roughly the size METR 2026 found (maintainer merge rate about one-third to one-half of grader pass rate). Refuted if the gap is within a few points, in which case F5 can be sampled less often. [N]

## 11. What is strong, moderate, weak, unknown (updated)

- **Strong (formal or replicated):** the Goodhart variants and their formal structure; proxy overoptimization in RLHF (Gao, in a synthetic setting); that unhackable proxies are rare (Skalse); the quantilizer theorem (Taylor, per decision); Kohavi/Hohnhold-style long-horizon holdouts (two companies: Google, and Pandora); minimal-criterion and QD archive results in robotics and games; the existence of in-context reward hacking in LLM loops (Pan 2024).
- **Moderate:** archive-based self-improving coding agents (DGM; one lab, fixed benchmarks, plus the HGM mismatch finding); reward hacking in frontier agents (METR 2025 blog; Anthropic 2025 in a deliberately constructed setting); engagement versus stated preference (Milli 2025); AI answer verifiability and attribution gaps (Liu, Tow, EBU); the test-pass versus merge gap (METR 2026; Wang 2025); developer perception gap (METR RCT, n = 16); LLM-judge gameability (Zheng 2024; One Token 2025); thresholded alignment (Moskovitz 2023; SITAlign).
- **Weak or absent:** North Star frameworks; balanced scorecard effect sizes; any specific exploration percentage (including 70/20/10); any evidence on LLM-agent satisficing-then-diversify in a product; KL regularisation as a practical Goodhart defence (contested, and its catastrophic failure mode is theoretical so far); DORA causal claims; single-author 2026 preprints on judge hacking and deterministic guardrails; the Heitzig aspiration-set line.
- **Unknown for Choir specifically:** whether owner value rises with archive diversity (H8); whether audit sampling stays undetectable (H12); how fast class A feedback leaks into learning the scorer (H13); whether commit-first scoring helps (H14).
- **Biggest practical risk** [K]: the withheld-signal design costs learning signal. The class A / class C split bets that mechanical and prediction-error feedback is rich enough. If agents plateau on quality, loosen class C (delayed, aggregated) before touching class A. DGM's hidden-checker result is the one piece of direct evidence on the hiding side; there is no published evidence on how much learning is lost by it.
