A bit more context:

I see Choir as a general-purpose technology built to serve my own
demand, and only then become a real business. That is, Choir is an
automatic computer rather than giving a chat-interfaced agent a
computer as most agent products do. And my own demand is for a better
way to do complex R&D, to consume and communicate ideas. So, Choir
rests on some divisive bets — against chat-shaped agents, against the
agent-as-replacement-worker framing — which could very well be wrong.
But if they're right, Choir would be very important, and should yield
great products and business models.

In December, I built the first prototype of a computer that updates its
own code and can generate apps on demand. Making that real (secure,
good UX, multitenant, minimizing failure, auditable event log, safe
recovery, no data loss) has been and still is a major undertaking.
Similarly, I first demoed the automatic newspaper in June. Integrating
it into Choir is still pending. I think I'm only a couple weeks away
from truly landing them both, but my time estimates have thus far been
optimistic. Until the new runtime and learning system land, my priority
is still going from prototype to production. But once they land,
go-to-market becomes P0.

Once the system is fully operational, it will take some calibration time
to understand exactly what buyers (in finance? or as a tool for other
data products?) want that Choir uniquely provides. I'm thinking to focus
on long-tail and less established data sources, especially non-English
language media, social media content, and multimedia (podcasts, video)
which I believe is less crowded and provides more marginal signal to
existing data buyers.

I would love to get your feedback on all this. I know I have a great
deal of work ahead of me, and I'd like to be coachable and receptive to
learning from experienced operators and fundraisers to position Choir
for success.
