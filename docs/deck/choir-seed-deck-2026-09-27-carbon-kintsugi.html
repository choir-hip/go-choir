Chat is a command line. You type a command, text comes back, and the state stays hidden. You learn the incantations — prompting is command syntax.

The storyDraft — founder to rewrite

I wanted agents to make work that compounds. I got drafts that regenerate slop.

Summer 2025
Built an AI ghostwriter because I wanted to be prolific. Models stitched together by hand; the output beat any single model.
The turn
Building it, I realized I did not want a ghostwriter. With the right tools — research, citation, analysis, understanding, writing, multimedia — I could produce quantity and quality myself.
The failed rebuild
Tried to rebuild it on an agent-graph framework. Agents do not follow a flowchart; they discover the workflow as they go.
The realization
Why give agents a computer when you can center the computer and give it agents. First prototype on localhost in about twenty minutes.
The chat loop
correction → regeneration → new slop
Talking to chat is easier than editing, so corrections go through regeneration. Each version fixes one thing and breaks another — nothing compounds.
The document loop
correction → revision → the work compounds
The edit is the message. The correction lands in the state and steers what happens next.
Professional poker, then AI since 2015.

The differenceDraft — founder to rewrite

Everyone sells compute. Choir owns state.

Every agent brings its own sandbox — and the sandbox is thrown away.
The sandbox crowd
30+ agent-VM and cloud-computer companies ship essentially the same product: a disposable machine per run.
What gets kept
The run ends, and the accumulated state stays inside the vendor’s silo. What the user learned — and what the agents learned — does not survive the vendor.
The objection
Memory import and export is arriving. Memory is a summary of what an assistant learned about you. Choir holds the work itself — versioned, and still being worked on by agents from any vendor.
The proof
Put a Markdown or PDF file into your Choir over MCP, then work on it from ChatGPT or Meta Muse. It stays live — versioned at the user level and the platform level, and still being worked on by the multi-agent system.

The engineDraft — founder to rewrite

A computer made of agents.

The harness
Natively multi-agent, in Go. Desks with bounded capabilities — Texture, Research, Engineering, Management, Mail — each a recursive language model with its own context and imports. No root agent; coordination lives in the state.
Precommitment records
Agents commit to predictions before acting; outcomes resolve them. The same log is the learning signal, the audit trail, and the reputation record. Supervision is a property of the log, not a chat window.
Model-neutral
Cheap open-weight models for volume, frontier models where the stakes are highest. The state outlives every model it passes through.
External validation — Harvey, September 2026: on 50 held-out diligence data rooms, a recursive harness lifted rubric pass rate from 23.3% to 62.4%, and per-room model cost fell to ~$7 against ~$18 for a standard tool loop.

AutoradioDraft — founder to rewrite

The real platform shift is voice.

Alexa and Siri were voice command lines: command, response, no state. Autoradio is a living station you steer by interrupting and recording your own takes.
After voice CLIs
Amazon's devices business lost more than $25B over five years; Apple pushed Siri's personal-context features back a year; Google replaced Assistant with Gemini for Home, calling the old one bound to rigid, specific commands.
Call-in radio, with provenance
Two-way radio has a proven social form — call-in radio built communities. Autoradio is call-in radio with provenance, where listeners’ takes become part of the record.
Delivery
A native mobile app plus an app inside the web desktop. Mobile puts Choir on the home screen, in the car, and on walks — where the daily habit forms.
Radio holds 32% of daily audio listening time and 55% of in-car audio time; 167M Americans listen to podcasts monthly. Edison Research / SSRS, 2026.
Generate the visuals. Preserve the voice.

Why nowDraft — founder to rewrite

Agents are everywhere. Their work belongs to no one.

Meta Muse shipped September 8 and went to #1 in the App Store; Town raised $55M; Instinct is reported in talks at a ~$10B valuation. Anthropic's Orbit and OpenAI's Aeon are reported but unannounced. Each keeps the user's work in the vendor's silo.

The question is not whether agents arrive. It is whose computer they work on.
