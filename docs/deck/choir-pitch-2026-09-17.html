Phase 1 · the technology

The automatic computer.

A persistent computer that works continuously and develops itself under owner supervision — a novel multi-agent architecture.

An RLM (recursive language model, cf. Zhang et al., MIT) runs long-horizon work by spawning sub-agents as asynchronous calls. PICL keeps learning weightless: committed predictions resolve into provenance-linked records — big-data sense-making without weight updates. Every change is a typed event; every surface is a deterministic projection.

Phase one is the technology, not the product — the moat everything else runs on.

Phase 3 · the upshot

The automatic radio.

A voice layer on top of the newspaper — a more advanced podcast player you can talk to.

An AI DJ traverses the live object graph and builds the queue: clips and full-length podcast episodes play natively; public and private documents are read aloud. Users record their own takes with voice and distribute them — internally or publicly.
Not a chatbot
Broadcast, not request-response. The DJ is a persistent agent — calibrating, mixing live sources on a schedule.
Not a podcast tool
A continuous station with a programming director — "what's on now" is a projection of live state, not a generated file.

The deployment answer

Your data stays home.

The go-to-market asks organizations to operate on their proprietary IP — which means exposing private data to the AI platforms they use. Choir answers two ways.
Managed
We operate it under zero-data-retention policies — cloud or open models, your choice.
Open source
Run the Choir OS yourself — with or without our consulting — for maximum privacy and control.
The offering spans the gamut: the hobbyist pays nothing; above hobbyist, the data products carry the subscription — the global object graph (a continuously updated OSINT index with provenance) and the box score (reputation statistics above it). PICL weightless learning makes open source a community-driven gym — and the deployment answer for every org that can't ship its data to someone else's cloud.
